1. Four missing outcomes are being counted as known noncompletion Analysis decision

Participants and Results, page 1

Current draft

Follow-up outcomes were observed for all 80 recruited students.

Fictional manuscript, p. 1
Current draft

Forty-eight students completed the course (60.0%), while the remaining 32 did not complete it.

Fictional manuscript, p. 1

What needs to change and why

The fictional dataset contains 80 recruited students, but only 76 have a recorded follow-up outcome: 48 completed the course and 28 did not. Four outcomes are missing. The original calculation counts every value other than an observed completion as noncompletion, turning those four unknown outcomes into four additional failures.

The denominator depends on the question being answered. Among students with a recorded outcome, completion is 48/76 = 63.2%. Among everyone recruited, documented completion is 48/80 = 60.0%. Both are useful descriptions, but 60.0% does not establish that the other 40.0% failed. If all four missing students completed the course, actual completion across the full cohort would be 65.0%; if none did, it would be 60.0%.

Results — completion among observed outcomes

Among the 76 students with recorded outcomes, 48 completed the course (63.2%) and 28 did not (36.8%). Four of the 80 recruited students had no recorded follow-up outcome.

Option B: keep all 80 recruits in the denominator and distinguish unknown outcomes.
Current draft

Forty-eight students completed the course (60.0%), while the remaining 32 did not complete it.

Results — documented completion across the recruited cohort

Among all 80 recruited students, 48 had documented course completion (60.0%), 28 had documented noncompletion (35.0%), and four had no recorded follow-up outcome (5.0%). Actual completion across the full cohort was therefore between 60.0% and 65.0%.

Implementation

  1. Preserve a missing follow-up value as missing. Code known completion and known noncompletion separately; do not turn an unobserved outcome into a known failure.
  2. Choose which descriptive question the Results paragraph answers and use the corresponding complete replacement. The Participants correction applies under either option.
  3. Use the same denominator and missingness description in the abstract, tables and figures. If the goal is actual completion across all recruits, seek the missing outcomes or report the bounds.
